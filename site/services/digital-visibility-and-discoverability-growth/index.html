Marketing & Sales

Help the right customers find your business online.

When people need a service like yours, can they find clear, useful information about what you do? BGI Solution helps you review how potential customers discover your business and where your website content or business listings may leave important questions unanswered.

The aim is to identify sensible improvements based on your audience, services and available information. We agree what to review, which changes to prioritize and how progress could be measured before work begins.

When this service may help

People search for your services but cannot easily understand your offer. Important service details, answers or next steps may be missing or hard to find.

  • Your website does not reflect what customers ask.Page titles, service explanations or supporting information may not match the needs behind relevant searches.
  • Your online business information is inconsistent or incomplete.Appropriate listings may need clearer service descriptions, contact details or other verified facts.
  • You want to know what to improve first.There are many possible changes, but you need a practical order and a way to check what happens after they are made.

Start with your customer and their question

Tell us whom you want to reach, which services matter most and where customers seem to have trouble finding or understanding you. We can discuss the site, content, listings and records that are relevant to your situation.

This service focuses on being easier to find and understand. Creating a new website, managing campaigns, publishing content or operating ongoing reporting is included only when those activities are explicitly agreed. Search rankings, traffic and inquiry volumes cannot be guaranteed.

How the visibility review can work

The review starts with the people you need to reach and the information they are looking for. These four stages describe a possible approach; your proposal confirms the reviewed channels, deliverables, responsibilities and depth.

1. Understand the audience and search needs

Clarify your priority services, intended customers, relevant locations where you actually operate, and the questions people ask when comparing providers. Identify the actions a useful page should help visitors take.

2. Review what customers can find

Examine the agreed website pages and appropriate business listings. Look for missing explanations, outdated or inconsistent information, unclear navigation, weak page-to-question fit and technical issues that require further checks.

3. Prioritize useful improvements

Group the gaps by customer value, likely effort, evidence and dependencies. Explain which content, page or listing changes deserve attention first and which assumptions should be checked before implementation.

4. Define how to review progress

Agree the available starting records, relevant discovery and website measures, the review period and who will check them. Explain what can be measured, what cannot be reliably attributed and which decisions the findings should support.

Make the next action clear

The result is a defined starting point, not an overwhelming checklist. You can use the agreed priorities with your own team, request scoped implementation, or connect the findings to another service when that work is needed.

What you can receive

Being found is only useful when the person can understand the offer and choose a next step.

Customer question to search result, useful page or listing, and clear inquiry path.

Two proposed outputs help you decide what to change and how to review the work. Your proposal identifies the included channels, depth, file formats, implementation responsibilities and review points.

Visibility Improvement Plan

A prioritized explanation of relevant gaps in website content and approved business listings. It can show which customer question a page should answer, what is missing or confusing, the recommended change and why that change matters.

The plan separates quick content corrections from changes that depend on development work, additional evidence, business approvals or access to third-party platforms. It identifies which recommendations are ready to act on and which require further checks.

Measurement Plan

A practical outline of what to observe after the agreed changes are used. Depending on available records and approved tools, measures may include relevant search discovery, visits to important pages and actions such as inquiries.

The plan defines the starting point, measurement method, review period and person responsible. It also explains data gaps, attribution limits and why changes in visits or inquiries cannot always be credited to one improvement.

More details

Project details

Scope, deliverables and examples, for when you want the specifics.

Choose a realistic scope

An improvement plan is not the same as completing every change. The proposal states which items BGI Solution will write, build, update, test or hand over, and which activities will be done by your team or another provider.

If the work requires content production, a redesigned site, listing access, analytics configuration or ongoing optimization, those items must be expressly included in the agreed scope.

Review evidence without overstating outcomes

Search and discovery depend on multiple factors outside any one project. We use available evidence to set priorities and review progress while explaining uncertainty. We do not promise search positions, visitor numbers, customer leads or sales.

Illustrative business situation

A local service company has several offerings, but its pages are hard to navigate

Imagine a business whose customers frequently ask whether a particular service is available in their area. Its website groups several services on one page, and the next step for contacting the business is not clear.

A scoped review could compare the intended customers' questions with the existing pages and approved service information. It might recommend clearer page headings, separate explanations for priority services, consistent listing details and a visible inquiry path.

The Visibility Improvement Plan would record the proposed changes and dependencies. The Measurement Plan could define which page visits and inquiry actions to review after approved changes are implemented. This example asserts no measured ranking, traffic or sales improvement.

FAQ

Questions about online visibility

Can’t find your answer? Ask us directly.

Is this the same as building a new website?

Not necessarily. We can discuss improvements to an existing website and its information. If new pages, a redesign or development work are needed, your proposal will explain whether those production tasks are included.

Can you guarantee higher search rankings or more inquiries?

No. Rankings, visits and inquiries are influenced by competition, search systems, business demand, site conditions and other factors. We agree useful work and measures, but do not guarantee these outcomes.

Will you update our website and business listings?

Only when implementation is part of the agreed scope. The review can identify priorities without changing anything. Updates require the relevant access, verified business facts, approvals and agreed responsibilities.

Do you also write articles or manage advertising?

This service focuses on relevant discovery and website or listing gaps. A content program, advertising campaign or ongoing publishing can be discussed as separate work or specifically included in the proposal.

What if we do not have analytics or reliable reports?

We can discuss what information is available and what can responsibly be measured. The plan will identify missing records and any setup that would be required; it will not claim a verified baseline where none exists.

How do we decide price and timing?

They depend on how many pages and listings are in scope, the available information, required changes, review arrangements and any implementation included. We agree deliverables, price and expected schedule before starting.
