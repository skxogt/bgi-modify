AI Readiness & AI Growth Enablement

AI may be worth exploring when a task is clear and its results can be checked. We start with the work people do today, the information it uses and the outcome the business wants. Then we examine whether AI is suitable, what must remain under human control and what would make a small pilot worthwhile.

What an AI readiness project can include

Workflow and use-case review. Describe the task, current steps, pain points and candidate AI uses. Compare the options with the expected benefit, feasibility and available evidence in mind.

Information and access assessment. Identify the data, systems and permissions a proposed use would need. Record missing, sensitive or unreliable information and dependencies that may prevent safe testing.

Risk and human-oversight checklist. Consider errors, privacy, security, accuracy, review duties and where a person must approve, correct or stop the work. The level of review depends on the use case.

Pilot plan and evaluation criteria. Set out a limited test, who will review outputs, what good performance looks like, the evidence to collect and when to stop, change or expand the pilot.

These are possible outputs, not a fixed bundle. Your proposal confirms the included assessment, any pilot support, deliverables and approval steps. Building, integrating or operating an AI system is separate unless explicitly included.

What we may need from you

Describe the task, who performs it, what tools or information it uses and what makes it difficult today. Share examples of acceptable and unacceptable outputs if you have them. We discuss what information is necessary and how access will be handled before asking for private or customer records.

What readiness means here

A positive assessment is not a promise that AI will produce reliable results in every situation. It helps you decide whether a limited pilot is justified, what needs to be fixed first and who will be responsible for reviewing its results.

Managed Growth Optimization & Intelligence Service

When a growth activity is already running, it can be hard to know which change deserves attention. This service establishes a defined review process: understand the current picture, choose a priority, agree a change or test, then examine what happened before deciding on the next step.

What an ongoing improvement engagement can include

Current-system and baseline review. Identify the website, marketing activity, customer process or other agreed system under review. Define the starting measures, available records, data gaps and known limits on interpretation.

Improvement priorities. Maintain an agreed list of issues or opportunities to assess. Explain the reason for each priority, the evidence behind it and any dependencies before work begins.

Authorized change or test plan. Describe the proposed adjustment, intended result, approval owner, required work, review window and measures. Changes are made only within agreed access and authority.

Review report and decision record. Compare the results with the starting position where valid, document unexpected effects and uncertainty, then record whether to keep, adjust, reverse or investigate a change further.

The proposal defines whether this is a one-time review or continuing service, how often reviews take place and which reporting, analysis, testing or implementation activities are included. It also specifies who owns each system and approves changes. Advertising spend, subscriptions and third-party fees are not assumed to be included.

What we may need from you

Tell us what is already operating, why you want it to work better, what measures you have and which teams or providers control the systems. We agree a useful baseline and access arrangements before reviewing business or customer data.

What results can tell you

A change in a metric does not always prove why it happened. Seasonality, customer mix, tracking gaps and other activity can affect results. Reviews should explain these limits, not present every change as a success.

How we agree the work

1. Start with the decision. Tell us the AI use you want to examine or the existing system you want to improve. Describe the problem in plain language and what you need to decide next.

2. Check the available information. Review existing work, systems and records. Identify gaps, access needs and any privacy or technical constraints before setting the scope.

3. Agree the outputs and responsibilities. Confirm the deliverables, review points, required approvals, price and expected schedule. For continuing work, agree the reporting frequency and change permissions.

4. Review evidence before taking the next step. Discuss what the findings or test results show, where they are uncertain and what should happen next. Any additional implementation or new scope is agreed separately.

Illustrative situations

These scenarios describe possible project scopes. They are not client cases, records of completed AI deployments or evidence of achieved results.

A team wants AI to help draft routine replies

A readiness review could map the inquiry types, the approved source information and which messages need a person to check them. A limited pilot plan could set criteria for accuracy, escalation and stopping the test before wider use.

A service website receives inquiries, but quality is unclear

An improvement engagement could review existing inquiry information and follow-up steps, define what a suitable inquiry means and identify one change to test. The review would report what can be concluded and where tracking or sample size limits confidence.

Clear boundaries protect useful work

AI work does not automatically include software development, model training, tool subscriptions or autonomous operation. Optimization work does not automatically include advertising, website production or unrestricted system changes. Each project agreement states what is included and who remains accountable for decisions and access.

Questions about these services

Do we need AI to improve our business systems?

No. Managed improvement can focus on a website, lead process or another existing system without AI. AI readiness is a separate service for deciding whether a particular task is suitable for AI.

Can we start with an AI idea if our data is incomplete?

Yes, we can discuss it. The readiness review identifies which information is necessary and whether important gaps prevent a useful pilot. Some ideas may need simpler process or data improvements first.

Will an AI readiness review include building the tool?

Not automatically. The proposal states whether the work covers assessment, a pilot plan, pilot support or any technical development. Integration, deployment and ongoing operation require explicitly agreed responsibilities.

What does “managed” mean for ongoing optimization?

It means the engagement has agreed review activities, a schedule and responsibilities. The proposal defines which systems are covered, who approves changes and whether BGI Solution will implement or only recommend them.

How will you know whether a change helped?

We agree starting measures, a review period and a way to record what changed. Findings describe the available evidence and other factors that may affect the result. Improvements or financial returns are not guaranteed.

What about sensitive business or customer information?

We discuss what is required, why it is needed and how access will be managed before information is shared. Privacy, permission and security requirements must be agreed for the specific tools and work involved.

Can we begin with only one defined problem?

Yes. You can start with one AI use case or one system that needs review. If another service becomes useful, we explain the separate scope before any additional work begins.

Tell us what you want to assess or improve

Tell us the task, workflow or growth system you have in mind, what is happening now and what you would like to understand. We can discuss the right starting point and the information needed to define the work.
