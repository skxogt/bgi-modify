How a trust and reputation review can work

The project begins with real customer concerns and the information your business can support. These four stages describe a possible approach, not a fixed package; the agreed brief defines what is included.

1. Identify customer questions and concerns

Review the questions people ask before contacting you or purchasing. Examine the important claims on your website and other agreed materials, then identify where customers may need clearer evidence or explanations.

2. Check available proof and permissions

Look at relevant existing records and materials, such as approved service information, documented processes, genuine feedback or work examples. Check sources, accuracy, dates, permissions and any limitations before recommending public use.

3. Plan clearer trust information

Match customer questions with valid evidence. Identify unsupported or unclear statements, missing explanations and opportunities to present accurate proof more usefully. Record which changes need fact-checking or approval.

4. Define a responsible feedback workflow

Agree how customers may be invited to leave genuine feedback, how comments are reviewed and answered, who takes responsibility and when complaints or sensitive issues need escalation. Define any platform work and ongoing activity separately.

Make the next decision easier

The result can help you choose which trust improvements to address first, which claims require more support and how to handle feedback consistently. Any content production, publication or recurring reputation management must be part of the agreed scope.

What you can receive

Trust grows from usable evidence, not from decorative badges.

Evidence inventory grouped as verified, permission needed, missing, or outdated.

Three proposed outputs turn a broad trust concern into evidence you can review, information you can improve and a practical way to handle customer feedback. The proposal defines which outputs and tasks are included.

Evidence Inventory

An organized record of the information that could support important customer questions or public claims. It identifies the source, date or context, what the evidence can reasonably show and where that proof might be useful.

The inventory also notes gaps, disputed or outdated information, permissions and any material that is not suitable to publish. An item is not an approved public claim merely because it appears in the inventory.

Trust and Proof Improvement Plan

A prioritized list of changes that could help customers assess your business. It can recommend clearer service explanations, appropriately supported statements, better proof placement or removal of claims that cannot be confirmed.

The plan explains why each change matters, what evidence supports it, what still needs checking and who must approve updates before publication. Actual writing, design and publication are defined separately.

Review and Reputation Workflow

A practical process for requesting genuine customer feedback, responding to it and escalating issues. It identifies who asks, who responds, which channels are relevant and when a concern needs human review.

The workflow should follow the rules of the platforms used and protect customer information. It does not promise positive ratings, review removal or continuous monitoring unless those activities are specifically scoped.

FAQ

Questions about trust and reputation

Can’t find your answer? Ask us directly.

Can you create testimonials or five-star reviews for us?

No. Customer stories and reviews must reflect genuine experiences. We can help you assess existing material and discuss a responsible way to request feedback, with appropriate permissions and platform rules.

Will this service improve our online rating?

We cannot guarantee a rating or how customers will respond. The work focuses on accurate information, genuine feedback processes and handling concerns consistently. Any outcome review depends on agreed measures and available records.

Can you publish customer stories or work examples?

That can be discussed when production is in scope. We need approved facts and any permissions or usage rights required. A proposed example cannot be presented as completed client work.

Do you remove negative reviews or manage complaints?

The scope may include a response or escalation process. Removal decisions belong to the relevant platforms and applicable rules. Ongoing monitoring, responses and issue handling need explicit agreement and named responsibilities.

What if we do not have customer reviews or other proof yet?

We can begin with accurate information your business can document, such as service scope, processes and policies. Evidence gaps will be recorded; we will not fill them with invented stories or unsupported credentials.

How are the price and schedule determined?

They depend on the information to review, proof and permission checks, channels involved, requested materials and any ongoing work. We agree the outputs, price and expected schedule before the project begins.
