How a website or asset project takes shape

A practical project starts with its purpose, not a template. The work can follow four stages, with the agreed brief setting the deliverables, approvals and technical boundaries.

1. Agree the brief

Identify the intended audience, what they need to understand and the action the finished material should support. Confirm the pages or assets, available business facts, features, formats, reviewers and delivery platform.

2. Plan the content and design

Organize clear messages and an appropriate page or document structure. Review proposed layouts, accessible reading order, mobile behavior and any interactions before moving into final production.

3. Produce and review

Create the agreed website pages or materials. Review them with the business for factual accuracy, usable design, specified features, required permissions and feedback at the planned stages.

4. Check and hand over

Check the work against the brief and deliver the agreed files or accessible handover materials. Record completed checks, unresolved issues and any setup or publication actions still assigned to the client or another provider.

Know what the handover includes

The project agreement defines what is built, which files are supplied, who approves them and what testing is included. A production handover is not a promise that a site has launched or achieved results.

FAQ

Questions about websites and business materials

Can’t find your answer? Ask us directly.

Can we start with a website or materials project?

Yes. Share what you need, who will use it and the purpose it should serve. Wider research or growth strategy is separate work if needed.

Can you improve an existing website or file?

Share the current material and desired changes. The review confirms what can be reused and whether the platform, source files and rights support the requested work.

Will you host or publish the finished website?

Only when those activities are included in the agreement. Domains, hosting, platform subscriptions, launch and ongoing technical support are separately assigned.

What files and features are included?

That depends on the brief. It specifies the pages or assets, file formats, responsive layouts, forms, integrations, testing and handover instructions being delivered.

Who owns the completed work?

The agreement defines ownership and usage rights for the commissioned materials and any third-party images, fonts, software or other licenses.

Can you guarantee more visitors or inquiries?

No. Clear content and useful design may help people understand and act, but traffic and inquiries depend on many factors. Search visibility and ongoing optimization are separate scopes.
