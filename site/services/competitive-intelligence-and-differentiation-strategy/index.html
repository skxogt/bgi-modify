Market Research

Understand your competition and what sets you apart.

When customers consider your business, what other choices do they have? How do those alternatives compare with your offer? BGI Solution helps you examine the competitive landscape and consider meaningful ways to stand apart.

We can review relevant competitors, substitutes and customer choices, compare what the available evidence shows, and develop differentiation options for you to evaluate. The aim is to support a clearer business decision, not to promise an advantage that has not been tested.

How the competitive review works

A side-by-side view can make differences clearer, provided every comparison has a source.

Comparison grid for offers, audiences, proof, and customer experience, with space for unknowns.

The review follows the decision at hand. These four stages show a possible approach; the sources, comparisons and strategic work are agreed before the project begins.

1. Define the market and alternatives

Agree the offer, intended customers, geographic or industry boundaries and the decision you need to make. Identify relevant direct competitors and other ways customers may solve the same problem. A useful comparison does not assume that every similar-looking business is a true alternative.

2. Compare relevant evidence

Review available offers, positioning, stated features, buying experience and other agreed comparison criteria. Record where information came from, when it was observed and what it can reasonably support. Separate a public claim by a competitor from independent evidence that the claim is accurate.

3. Explore meaningful differences

Examine how your offer compares and where customers may see a relevant difference. Consider whether a proposed distinction is useful to the intended buyer, credible and something your business can deliver. Note alternatives and assumptions that require further checking.

4. Explain the options and next checks

Organize the comparison and differentiation options so you can review the trade-offs. Identify which ideas have evidence behind them, which remain hypotheses and what would need to be tested before changing your positioning or message.

What the review should help you decide

The work should make the competitive choices easier to understand: which alternatives matter, what customers may value, which differences appear credible and where more evidence is needed before committing to a change.

What you can receive

The proposed outputs connect competitor evidence to strategic choices. Your proposal confirms which deliverables are included, the scope of each comparison and any additional validation.

Competitor Map

A structured view of relevant direct competitors, substitutes and alternatives using criteria agreed for your business decision. It may compare offers, customer needs addressed, positioning or buying experience where reliable information exists.

The map records evidence sources, observation dates and important gaps. It gives you a way to see similarities and potential differences without treating a marketing claim, estimate or incomplete observation as a verified fact.

Differentiation Options

A set of possible positioning choices that explain how your business might distinguish its offer in ways the intended customer cares about. Each option identifies its rationale, supporting evidence, potential trade-offs and assumptions to validate.

These are choices to evaluate, not guaranteed competitive advantages or approved advertising claims. The final message needs to match your actual offer and be reviewed or tested before broader use.

What supports a useful comparison
  • Relevant alternatives.Explain why each competitor or substitute is included and which customer choice it represents.
  • Dated, traceable sources.Record what was observed, where it came from and how changes or missing information may affect the finding.
  • Clear evidence limits.Distinguish observed facts, competitors' own statements, estimates and interpretations.

Any interviews, message tests, website changes, offer redesign or continuing competitor monitoring require an explicit agreement. A one-time competitor map does not imply live surveillance or guaranteed market intelligence.

Illustrative differentiation situation

Several providers appear to offer the same service

Imagine a professional service business whose website describes its work in similar terms to several alternatives. The owner is unsure which differences matter to potential buyers or whether the current message accurately explains the offer.

A project could compare selected public offers, stated service features and decision information using agreed criteria. A competitor map could show where offerings appear similar and where information is missing. Differentiation options might explore more specific customer needs, service processes or delivery characteristics, but only if the business can support those distinctions.

The business would then decide whether to validate an option and separately commission revised website content or sales materials. The comparison alone would not demonstrate increased demand or sales.

FAQ

Common questions

Can’t find your answer? Ask us directly.

Do we need to provide a competitor list?

Not necessarily. Tell us which customer decision or market you want to understand. We can discuss how to identify relevant competitors and substitutes within the agreed scope.

Can you compare companies outside our city or industry?

We can discuss the locations, industries and alternatives relevant to your decision. The proposal defines research coverage and the information needed; no particular geographic coverage is assumed.

How do you make sure a comparison is fair?

We use agreed criteria, record source dates and distinguish a competitor's own statements from other evidence. Important limitations and missing information remain visible rather than being filled with guesses.

Will you choose our new positioning for us?

The work can present and evaluate differentiation options. You decide which direction to pursue, and any customer validation, message testing or implementation is agreed separately.

How is this different from customer or offer research?

Customer & Market Research focuses on customer groups, needs and buying decisions. This service compares alternatives and develops differentiation strategy. Offer & Messaging Improvement focuses on how your own offer is explained and structured.

Are market-share estimates or ongoing monitoring included?

Not automatically. Reliable market-share figures may not be available, and recurring monitoring is a separate activity. Your proposal specifies any estimates, research methods, review schedule and associated limits.
