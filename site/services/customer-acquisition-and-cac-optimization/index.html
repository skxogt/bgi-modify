Service SVC-14

Understand what it costs to win a customer.

More inquiries do not always mean more customers. BGI Solution helps you examine what your business spends to attract new customers, what the available records show and which parts of the process deserve a closer look.

Customer acquisition cost, or CAC, is the cost of winning a new customer under an agreed definition. We help you make the calculation understandable, identify gaps in the evidence and prioritize possible improvements. The project scope defines what will be analyzed, delivered, changed or tested.

When this service may help

You know what you spend, but not what it produces. Advertising, content or sales expenses are recorded, but new-customer counts and time periods are not connected clearly.

Some channels seem expensive. You want to compare acquisition activities, but costs, customer fit and sales timing differ from one channel to another.

Too much effort goes to poor-fit inquiries. The business may attract interest that does not match its offer, capacity or target customers.

The buying process has costly friction. Delays, unclear handovers or repeated work may consume resources before a prospect becomes a customer.

Start with a cost question you need to answer

Tell us which acquisition activities you use, what you currently measure and where the costs or results are unclear. We can discuss a focused review of the available data and the decisions the analysis needs to support.

This service examines acquisition economics and possible improvements. It does not automatically include running advertising campaigns, managing a sales team, implementing changes or guaranteeing lower costs.

How an acquisition cost review can work

We start by agreeing what counts as a new customer and which spending and time periods to include. The following stages describe a possible approach; actual methods, access and responsibilities are defined before work begins.

1. Define the customer and the cost

Agree what counts as a new customer, the business activity being evaluated and the period to review. List relevant costs, such as campaign spending and any included marketing or sales resources. Explain what is excluded so comparisons remain meaningful.

2. Connect spending with available results

Review permitted records for spending, inquiries, qualified opportunities and new customers. Check whether source tracking, customer counts and time periods can be linked reliably. Label estimates, missing costs and unclear attribution instead of treating them as facts.

3. Investigate possible sources of waste

Compare activities only where definitions and evidence support a fair comparison. Look for poor-fit inquiries, repeated steps, unnecessary spending and obstacles between interest and purchase. Separate observed patterns from possible causes that still need testing.

4. Prioritize changes and a review method

Rank practical options by likely relevance, evidence, feasibility and dependencies. Agree what may be changed, who would carry it out, how the starting position will be recorded and when the result can reasonably be reviewed.

Measure carefully before changing spending

A simple CAC calculation divides the agreed acquisition costs by the new customers counted over a suitable period. It is only as dependable as those definitions and records. Differences in sales cycles, customer value, timing and attribution can make direct channel comparisons misleading.

What you can receive

Breakdown of included spend and new customers won over the same stated period.

Two proposed outputs make the available economics easier to interpret and turn uncertainties into a practical improvement discussion. The agreed scope determines the format, analysis depth and any implementation or testing included.

Acquisition Cost Model

A structured view of the costs and new-customer counts included in the analysis. It states the measurement period, cost categories, customer definition, available sources and any estimates or exclusions before presenting CAC calculations.

Where records allow, the model may support comparisons between relevant channels or activities. It also notes differences in customer mix, timing and attribution that limit how confidently one cost can be compared with another.

Improvement Priorities

A ranked set of possible ways to improve how acquisition resources are used. Examples may include refining audience fit, reducing unnecessary steps, addressing qualification gaps or testing a clearer handover between marketing and sales.

Each priority can identify the issue, supporting evidence, assumed benefit, owner, dependency and a suggested way to test the change. These are improvement options, not proven savings or guaranteed performance results.

Implementation and measurement boundaries

The brief clarifies whether the work includes analysis only, recommendations, campaign adjustments, process changes, tracking configuration or follow-up measurement. Advertising purchases, CRM changes, customer outreach and ongoing management require separately agreed authorization and responsibilities.

Costs and customer counts may not line up neatly, especially with long buying cycles or incomplete source tracking. A reported CAC is an estimate when the inputs are incomplete. Any expected efficiency gain must be validated with actual results after a change.

A business sees inquiries but cannot explain the cost of new customers

What we may need from you

Share your target customers, acquisition channels, agreed definitions, permitted spending data and available customer counts. Where records contain personal or confidential information, confirm appropriate access, minimization, permissions and safeguards before providing them.

What we agree before starting

Business question and scope. Confirm which customers, channels, time periods and decisions the analysis needs to cover.

Cost and customer definitions. Agree included and excluded costs, what counts as a new customer, data sources and known timing or attribution problems.

Deliverables and ownership. Define the acquisition model, improvement priorities, review responsibilities and any authorized system or campaign changes.

Tests and terms. Set measures, assumptions, review periods, approval steps, confidentiality requirements, price, schedule and whether ongoing work is included.

Questions about acquisition cost and efficiency

What does CAC mean?

CAC stands for customer acquisition cost. It divides agreed costs by new customers counted over a suitable period. The model explains what is included and excluded.

Can you calculate CAC if our tracking is incomplete?

We review available inputs and identify missing expenses, uncertain customer counts or unclear attribution. Any resulting estimate is labeled, not presented as a verified figure.

Will you tell us which marketing channel is best?

We compare activities where the evidence supports it. Lower recorded cost does not always mean better customer fit or value; sales timing can distort comparisons.

Does the service include running ads or changing our CRM?

Not automatically. Ad spend, campaign execution, system changes and ongoing management require explicit scope and approval.

How would we know whether a proposed change helped?

If testing is agreed, we define a baseline, a change, review timing and measures. We document differences in demand, customer mix and tracking that limit interpretation.

Can you guarantee a lower CAC or more customers?

No. Costs depend on the market, offer, sales process, budget and data quality. The work can clarify economics and recommend tests, not promise savings or new customers.
